# References

Look at these before the Visuals pass and whenever you judge a Folio screen. Take their qualities, never their subject or layout, so every product still gets its own screen.

## Carter's screens

Folio in the Folio Sandbox workspace, 2026-11-05, with synthetic data. The sandbox strip under the top bar only appears in sandbox workspaces.

| File | What it shows | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [folio-today.jpg](refs/folio-today.jpg) | Today: date and greeting, four figures with their own trend lines, the setup checklist, the pipeline table | Quiet chrome, framed panels, one accent on the primary action and the checklist progress. Figures carry the moment. The greeting is content, so it stays even though pages carry no titles |
| [folio-leads.jpg](refs/folio-leads.jpg) | Leads board: stage columns with counts and totals, deal cards with contact, company, source and age | One card treatment repeated, stage dots as the only color, totals right aligned in tabular figures. No helper text anywhere |
| [folio-phone-today.jpg](refs/folio-phone-today.jpg) | Today on a phone, with the bottom tab bar | A phone layout, not a shrunken desktop. Same tokens, simpler stack |
| [folio-sign-in.jpg](refs/folio-sign-in.jpg) | Sign-in: a narrow form beside a painting, with a quiet credit | The painting is the one rich moment; everything else is a field and a button |
| [vermeer-geographer.jpg](refs/vermeer-geographer.jpg) | Johannes Vermeer, The Geographer, 1669 (public domain) | The sign-in painting. Window light, one blue robe, a map: the world Folio belongs to. Use it to judge warmth and restraint, not as decoration |

What they share: the richness lives in data and one painting, the chrome stays in the ramp, and nothing on screen explains the screen.
